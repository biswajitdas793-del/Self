# Claude AI Training Program - Trainer Guide
## Complete Instructions for Delivering the 5-Week Program

---

## PROGRAM OVERVIEW

**Duration:** 5 weeks, 2-3 hours per week  
**Format:** Interactive, hands-on instruction  
**Audience:** HR, Marketing, Sales, Customer Support, Operations, Finance, Management  
**Total Participants:** Up to 50 per cohort  
**Prerequisites:** Basic computer literacy, email, web browsing

## WEEK 1: AI & Claude Fundamentals
**Duration:** 2.5 hours | **Slides:** 40

### Session Structure
- Welcome & Icebreaker (10 min)
- AI Fundamentals Lecture (30 min)
- Claude Introduction (25 min)
- Features Walkthrough (30 min)
- Hands-On Exercise #1 (25 min)
- Q&A & Wrap-up (10 min)
- Exercise Debrief (20 min)

### Key Learning Objectives
By end of Week 1, participants should:
- Explain what AI and Generative AI are
- Understand how Large Language Models work
- Be comfortable navigating Claude's interface
- Complete their first Claude interactions
- Understand responsible AI principles

### Speaker Notes

**Opening (Slide 1-3)**
"Welcome to the Claude AI Training Program! Over the next five weeks, we're going to transform how you work. Claude isn't here to replace you—it's here to amplify your capabilities. Think of it as a highly educated colleague who never gets tired."

**AI Fundamentals (Slide 4-8)**
- Use metaphors: "AI is like training a very smart assistant by showing examples"
- Show real-world examples: recommendation engines, speech recognition, face unlock
- Distinguish between AI types: "AI is the umbrella, Machine Learning is the rain, Deep Learning is the specific drops"
- Generative AI is unique because it *creates* new content

**LLM Explanation (Slide 9-11)**
"Think of LLMs like an autocomplete on steroids. They've read billions of words and learned patterns about how language works. When you ask a question, Claude predicts the most likely next word, then the next, building your answer word by word. It's not magic—it's sophisticated pattern matching."

Story to tell: "GPT-1 was like a child learning to read. ChatGPT was like a well-educated adult. Claude 3 is like a PhD researcher who specializes in your field."

**Claude & Anthropic (Slide 12-15)**
- Anthropic focuses on safety first, unlike some competitors
- Constitutional AI means Claude is trained with values built-in
- Key differentiator: "Claude is designed to be helpful, harmless, and honest"
- Show: Different interfaces (web, API, plugins) and how to access them

**Features Deep Dive (Slide 16-22)**
Live demo is critical here:
1. Open claude.ai live during training
2. Create a project right then
3. Upload a sample PDF (have one ready)
4. Show how to ask questions about it
5. Demonstrate image upload and analysis
6. Show the context window explanation with visual

**Responsible AI (Slide 23-24)**
"With great power comes great responsibility. Claude is powerful, so we need to use it wisely. Some guidelines: never share passwords or sensitive personal data, always fact-check outputs before using them, remember that humans make final decisions on important matters."

**Hands-On Exercise (Slide 25-27)**
Lead the group through this together:
1. "Everyone go to claude.ai and create an account"
2. "Create your first project called 'Training'"
3. "Upload this PDF I'm sending to the chat"
4. "Ask Claude: 'What are the main points in this document?'"
5. Debrief: "What surprised you? What was easy? What was confusing?"

### Common Questions
- "Will Claude replace my job?" → "No, AI-fluent people will replace people who don't use AI. Learn it now and you'll be invaluable."
- "How does Claude know that?" → "It learned patterns from training data, but always verify important facts"
- "Is my data private?" → "Yes, your conversations are encrypted and don't train Claude"
- "Which Claude model should I use?" → "Sonnet is the best default—fast, capable, and cost-effective"

### Homework Assignment
"For next week, I want you to complete 20 different prompts in Claude. Five summarizations, five writing tasks, five analyses, five brainstorming sessions. Document what worked and what didn't. Bring your best 3 examples to Week 2."

### Assessment
Week 1 Quiz (10 minutes at end of session):
- 10 multiple choice questions
- 2 short answer questions
- Passing score: 70%
- Provide answer key

## WEEK 2: Prompt Engineering Mastery
**Duration:** 2.5 hours | **Slides:** 30

### Session Structure
- Week 1 Review & Quiz Debrief (15 min)
- Homework Share (15 min)
- Prompt Anatomy Lecture (30 min)
- Live Prompt Demonstrations (30 min)
- Hands-On Exercise #1: Prompt Rewriting (30 min)
- Hands-On Exercise #2: Build Prompt Library (20 min)
- Q&A & Wrap-up (10 min)

### Key Learning Objectives
By end of Week 2, participants should:
- Understand the elements of effective prompts
- Write specific, context-rich prompts
- Demonstrate role and context prompting
- Create 30+ reusable prompts for their role
- Know how to iterate and refine prompts

### Speaker Notes

**Week 1 Debrief (Slide 1-2)**
"Last week was foundational. Now we're getting into the secret sauce of AI—prompting. Good prompting can multiply your results by 10x. Bad prompting wastes time. This week, you'll become a prompt engineer."

**Prompt Anatomy (Slide 3-8)**
"Every good prompt has ingredients: Context (what Claude needs to know), Task (what you want done), and Format (how you want it). Let's look at three versions of the same request:

Bad: 'Write an email'
Good: 'Write a professional email to a prospect'
Great: 'Write a 3-paragraph professional email introducing our new product to a prospect in the tech industry. Tone: friendly but authoritative. Include a clear call-to-action.'

The difference? Specificity. More specific prompts get better results."

**Live Demonstrations (Slide 9-20)**
Do 5 live prompts in Claude during training:

1. Role Prompting Example:
   - Bad: "Summarize this market research"
   - Great: "You're a strategy consultant. Summarize this market research in a way that highlights strategic opportunities and threats for our company."

2. Context Prompting Example:
   - Provide background about the company
   - Ask Claude to analyze with that context
   - Show how output changes with context

3. Few-Shot Prompting Example:
   - Show 2-3 examples of desired format
   - Ask Claude to generate more in that style
   - Compare to without examples

4. Iterative Refinement Example:
   - Get an initial response
   - Say "Make this more concise"
   - Say "Add more specific examples"
   - Show how iteration improves results

5. Chain-of-Thought Example:
   - Ask a complex question straight
   - Ask the same question with "Show your reasoning"
   - Compare—second answer is more reliable

**Exercise #1: Prompt Rewriting (Slide 21-22)**
Provide 5 bad prompts. Have groups rewrite them. Examples:

Bad: "Write about marketing"
Better: "Write a 300-word blog post outline about AI in B2B marketing for decision-makers, with 5 main points and 2-3 supporting details per point"

Process:
1. Break into groups of 3-4
2. Provide prompts via chat
3. Have them write improved versions
4. Test both versions in Claude
5. Compare results
6. Discuss what improved quality

**Exercise #2: Build Library (Slide 23-26)**
This is the capstone of Week 2:

"Now you're going to build your personal prompt library. Think about your top 5 recurring tasks. For each, write a template prompt.

Example for an HR person:
- Task: Screen resumes for 5 key qualifications
- Template prompt:
  'I'm recruiting for a [ROLE] position. Key qualifications needed: [LIST]. Screen this resume and rate fit on a 1-10 scale with brief justification: [RESUME]'

Your library should have:
- 30+ prompts organized by category
- Each with: Purpose, Template, Example output
- Notes on variations and tips"

Assign homework: Come to Week 3 with 30-prompt library

### Common Questions
- "How long should prompts be?" → "Long enough to be clear, short enough to be clear. Usually 1-3 sentences. Longer if you need specific context."
- "Can I just copy prompts from the internet?" → "Yes, but customizing them for your specific context makes them better. Modify to match your needs."
- "How many examples do I need for few-shot prompting?" → "Usually 2-3. More than 5 becomes diminishing returns."

### Assessment
Homework review and discussion of prompt libraries

3. Privacy: Never share sensitive data

"Adoption is hard. People get comfortable with old ways of working. Here's the formula for successful change:

1. Build awareness: Show the value
2. Create early wins: Make it easy to see benefits
3. Train thoroughly: People need skills
4. Document workflows: Make it repeatable
5. Measure and communicate: Show the wins
6. Iterate and optimize: Keep improving

Some people will embrace Claude immediately. Others will be skeptical. Your job as leaders is to answer their concerns respectfully and show them what's possible."

- Documentation (20%): Can others replicate this?
- Presentation (20%): Was it engaging and professional?

"Problem: Writing 30 blog posts a month takes 60 hours. Solution: Use Claude with a template to draft, then humans edit. Results: 40 hours saved (67%), same quality. ROI: $2,400/month at $60/hour."

## DELIVERY TIPS FOR TRAINERS

### Technical Setup
- Have backup laptop with Claude already logged in
- Test all demos the morning of training
- Have sample documents ready to upload
- Have internet backup (mobile hotspot)
- Recording equipment for creating followup materials

### Engagement Strategies
- Use ice-breakers in each session
- Call on people by name
- Ask "what if" questions
- Have people try things themselves, don't just watch
- Celebrate early wins
- Address concerns seriously

### Pacing Guidance
- Go slower than you think: Let time for questions
- Build in buffers: Exercises always take longer than planned
- Flexible agenda: Respond to what group needs
- Recap key points multiple times in different ways
- Don't try to cover everything—depth over breadth

### Handling Questions
- "That's a great question, let's explore it"
- Invite group to answer: "Has anyone had this issue?"
- Honest when you don't know: "Let me find that answer for you"
- Write down questions you can't immediately answer
- Follow up via email after class

### Common Training Challenges & Solutions

**Challenge:** People already used to doing things manually, resistant to change
**Solution:** Show them the time savings with concrete numbers. Let them experience success before demanding adoption.

**Challenge:** Some people finish exercises quickly, others struggle
**Solution:** Have extension exercises ready. Pair fast finishers with those struggling.

**Challenge:** Wide range of technical comfort
**Solution:** Have IT support available. Pair tech-savvy people with less tech-comfortable people in exercises.

**Challenge:** People worry Claude will make them obsolete
**Solution:** "AI-fluent people won't be replaced. People who don't use AI will be replaced by people who do. Your value is in using AI effectively."

**Challenge:** Data privacy concerns
**Solution:** Clear policy: Don't upload sensitive data. Use sanitized examples. Show the encryption and security measures.

## EVALUATION & ROI TRACKING

### Metrics to Track
1. **Adoption:** % of trained employees using Claude monthly
2. **Usage:** Average uses per employee per week
3. **Time Savings:** Hours saved per week per user
4. **Quality:** Error rates, revision rates before/after Claude
5. **Satisfaction:** NPS score for training and tool
6. **ROI:** Actual calculated productivity gains

### Survey Questions
- How often do you use Claude weekly?
- What tasks do you use it for?
- How much time does Claude save you per week?
- Has Claude improved your work quality?
- How confident are you using Claude?
- Would you recommend Claude to a colleague?

### ROI Calculation Template
```
Time Saved Per Person Per Week: ___ hours
Weekly Salary ($/hour): $___.00
Weekly Value Per Person: $___.00
Number of People Trained: ___
Weekly Organizational Value: $___.00
Annual Value (×52): $___.00

Training Costs (license, trainer time, materials): $___.00
Annual ROI: (Annual Value - Training Costs) / Training Costs × 100%
```
